# Adaptive learning: University of Nairobi evidence base

**Research checked 21 October 2026.** The personalisation model follows the University of Nairobi Faculty of Law's published programme outcomes and themes. It treats a learner's selected year as a starting point for scaffolding—not as evidence of mastery or as a reliable course/unit list.

## What UON publishes

The official [Bachelor of Laws programme page](https://law.uonbi.ac.ke/admission-content-type/bachelor-laws-0) specifies a four-year LLB, programme goals, learning outcomes and delivery methods. Its stated goals include understanding the social, economic, political and cultural context of law; analytical and holistic problem-solving; critical thinking; attention to both legal doctrine and the business of law; and values associated with human rights, rule of law, sustainable development and constitutional governance. Outcomes include applying legal theories/rules in transdisciplinary context, evaluating regional and international legal orders, analysing technology's interaction with law, and becoming practice-ready, analytical problem-solvers, independent thinkers and effective communicators. It names four thematic areas: (1) financial and commercial law; (2) human rights, democracy, governance and law; (3) environmental law; and (4) international law.

The published page contains a fee table with year-by-year unit totals, but it does **not** provide a complete authoritative year-by-year subject sequence. Accordingly, this platform must not claim, for example, that a particular subject is a prescribed Year 2 or Year 3 UON unit unless that is verified from the learner's course guide or selected material. Year affects the degree of scaffolding and complexity only; the assistant asks for the current course/topic when it matters.

The official [Master of Laws page](https://law.uonbi.ac.ke/admission-content-type/master-laws-0) describes specialization in emerging areas at national, regional and international levels; critical grounding; research; nine taught course units, including three core and at least four in the selected thematic area; and a research project report equivalent to four course units. It lists six thematic areas: corporate/financial law; public international law and international relations; human rights; intellectual property and technology; environment and natural resources; and democracy, governance and law. Masters learners are prompted to provide their specialization rather than being placed on a fictitious single postgraduate syllabus.

The official [PhD in Law page](https://law.uonbi.ac.ke/admission-content-type/doctor-philosophy-law) emphasizes critical and theoretical approaches, law in social/economic/cultural/political context, advanced scholarly research, and communicating research to researchers, academics and wider audiences. It lists research interests spanning governance/democracy, finance, trade/investment, law in society, gender, international law, environment/natural resources, intellectual property, science/technology, and corporate governance. Doctoral guidance therefore foregrounds research framing, method, evidence, critical contribution and communication without presuming a topic.

UON's [Faculty of Law programme list](https://law.uonbi.ac.ke/basic-page/all-programmes-faculty-law) confirms the listed LLB, LLM, and doctoral programmes, including environmental law/policy alternatives. That variation reinforces the need to ask the learner's programme/specialization rather than treating every postgraduate student as the same course.

## How the platform applies this evidence

- The initial profile asks for programme/year and a separately chosen support style: **guided**, **balanced**, or **challenge**. The study style can be changed in Profile settings.
- Guided support breaks work into short steps, defines terms, demonstrates an example, then checks understanding; balanced support combines a clear structure and an independent exercise; challenge support expects more independent reasoning and surfaces counterarguments.
- LLB year 1 begins with foundations and explicit reasoning steps; year 2 builds contextual application; year 3 emphasizes synthesis, wider legal context and practical problem-solving; year 4 emphasizes independent research, synthesis and communication. These are progressively scaffolded learning recommendations derived from UON's programme outcomes—not claims about official UON unit order.
- LLM/PhD guidance is separate and prompts for specialization or research question. It does not label all postgraduates “advanced LLB” or assume a particular research subject.
- Cohort tags distinguish Library resources and assignment visibility. Untagged items intentionally remain shared; tag existing materials/assignments where a course or cohort restriction is intended. The system does not infer cohort from content text.
- Every educational AI request receives the saved level and support preference, including the Study Assistant, assignment helper, exam review, research writing, guided study, learning studio, legal process explanations, quiz/notes, and debate/game flows. Sensitive counselling and scheduling tools are excluded from educational calibration.

## Limitations

The UON public programme pages are programme overviews, not full course handbooks. The four LLB year descriptions are scaffold levels, not an official sequence of course units. The interface does not yet infer mastery from assessment performance; personalisation is based on profile choices and resource tags. Learners can adjust these choices at any time. Untagged library items are deliberately treated as shared until a human tags them.
